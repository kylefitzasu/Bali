Day 1

Hong Kong Island

Markets, Temples & The Peak

🥟
8:00 AM Breakfast

Tim Ho Wan

Start with the world's most affordable Michelin-starred dim sum. Order the legendary BBQ pork buns (baked, not steamed), har gow, and cheung fun. Budget ~$15 USD for two.

▾
💡

Arrive right at 8am when it opens — lines form fast. Inside the MTR Hong Kong Station.

🏯
10:00 AM Culture

Man Mo Temple

One of HK's oldest and most atmospheric temples on Hollywood Road. Massive incense coils hang from the ceiling — the smoke and scent are otherworldly. Free to enter.

▾
💡

Stroll Hollywood Road afterward — it's lined with antique shops selling jade, ceramics, and art.

🥧
11:30 AM Snack

Tai Cheong Bakery

A mandatory stop. These egg tarts are the stuff of legend — buttery, flaky crust with silky custard. Chris Patten, the last British Governor, was famously obsessed with them.

▾
💡

Get them hot out of the oven — ask staff when the next batch is ready.

⛰️
12:30 PM Iconic View

Victoria Peak

Take the historic Peak Tram up — the incline is dramatic and thrilling. At the top, skip the paid terrace and walk Lugard Road (free, 15 mins) for the most photogenic panoramic views of the harbor and skyline.

▾
💡

Bus 15 up, Peak Tram down is the local's move. Go on a clear day — call it a morning activity if you have flexibility.

🍻
3:30 PM Neighborhood

Lan Kwai Fong

HK's most famous bar and restaurant district. By day, grab a craft beer or cocktail at one of the terraces. A great spot to people-watch, recharge, and plan the evening.

▾
💡

The alleys are steeper than they look. Wear comfortable shoes.

⭐
7:00 PM Splurge Dinner

Lung King Heen

The world's first Chinese restaurant to earn 3 Michelin stars. Cantonese cuisine at its absolute peak — harbor views, impeccable dim sum, and seafood dishes you'll talk about for years. Book weeks in advance.

▾
💡

Request a harbor-view table when booking. The lunch dim sum is slightly more accessible price-wise if dinner is too steep.

Day 2

Kowloon Peninsula

Markets, Temples & Harbour Magic

🏮
9:00 AM Culture

Wong Tai Sin Temple

One of HK's grandest Taoist temples, famous for fortune-telling. Locals come to shake the kau cim sticks for readings. The colorful architecture, gardens, and koi ponds make this a photographer's dream.

▾
💡

Visit before 10am for a calmer, more local experience. Dress respectfully.

🛍️
11:00 AM Shopping

Ladies' Market, Mong Kok

A sprawling street market on Tung Choi Street — clothes, souvenirs, electronics, and trinkets. The real fun is bargaining. Start at half the asking price and work from there.

▾
💡

Nearby Fa Yuen Street ('Sneaker Street') is great if you're into trainers. Also check out the goldfish market just around the corner.

🍜
1:00 PM Lunch

Mong Kok Dai Pai Dong

Grab lunch at one of the open-air cooked food stalls in the area — char siu rice, wonton noodle soup, or congee. This is quintessential HK street food culture.

▾
💡

Look for places packed with locals — a queue is always a good sign. Budget ~$5-8 USD per person.

⛴️
3:00 PM Iconic Experience

Star Ferry

The most scenic $0.62 you'll ever spend. The 10-minute ferry crossing between Tsim Sha Tsui and Central has been running since 1888. The harbor views from the water are unmatched.

▾
💡

Take the upper deck for the best views and photos. Do it both ways — HK Island skyline from Kowloon is spectacular.

⭐
5:00 PM Waterfront Walk

Avenue of Stars

HK's answer to Hollywood's Walk of Fame — handprints of Jackie Chan, Bruce Lee, Jet Li, Michelle Yeoh, and more. The harbor promenade at golden hour is absolutely stunning.

▾
💡

Walk from the Star Ferry pier east along the promenade. Grab drinks at a harborfront bar to settle in for the light show.

🌙
8:00 PM Night Market

Temple Street Night Market

The most atmospheric night market in HK. Fortune tellers, street opera, seafood stalls, neon signs. Don't miss the dai pai dong at the south end for grilled oysters and clams.

▾
💡

Goes until 11pm. Bargain hard — the asking price is always inflated for tourists.

Day 3

Adventure Day

Dragon's Back, Sai Kung & Skyline Farewell

🥾
7:30 AM Hiking

Dragon's Back Trail

Named Asia's best urban hike. The ridge trail follows the spine of Hong Kong Island with 360° views of the South China Sea, Shek O village, and Big Wave Bay. About 8km, 2.5–3 hours.

▾
💡

End at Big Wave Bay beach — take a dip if the water's warm. Bring sunscreen, water, and sturdy shoes. Take bus 9 from Shau Kei Wan MTR.

🏖️
11:00 AM Hidden Gem

Shek O Village

A sleepy, charming fishing village just below Dragon's Back. Colorful colonial houses, a Thai restaurant or two, and a beautiful beach. Feels a world away from Central.

▾
💡

Rent a beach chair and decompress after the hike. The village has good casual restaurants for brunch.

🦀
1:30 PM Adventure Lunch

Sai Kung Seafood Street

Take a taxi or bus to Sai Kung — a fishing town on the New Territories coast. Pick live seafood straight from the tanks (prawns, crab, abalone, lobster), haggle a price, and a restaurant will cook it for you.

▾
💡

Compare prices between restaurants — the seafood cost is separate from the cooking fee. Budget ~$40–60 USD for a serious feast for two.

⛵
4:00 PM On the Water

Sai Kung Harbour Boat Trip

Small wooden kaido boats ferry you to hidden beaches and islands around the Sai Kung peninsula — some only accessible by water. Pure adventure with incredible coastal scenery.

▾
💡

Negotiate directly with the boat captains at the pier. A 2-hour trip to Sharp Island or Kiu Tsui Beach runs ~$100–150 USD for the boat.

🍸
7:30 PM Farewell Dinner

Aqua Restaurant

On the 17th floor of a Tsim Sha Tsui tower with floor-to-ceiling windows over Victoria Harbour. Japanese-Italian fusion, a world-class cocktail menu, and arguably the best skyline view in HK. The perfect final dinner.

▾
💡

Make a reservation and specifically request a window table. Order cocktails at the bar first — the bar views are also stunning.

Essential Tips

🚇
Octopus Card
Buy at any MTR station. Covers metro, buses, Star Ferry, and even 7-Eleven.
💰
Money
Carry HKD cash for markets and street food. Cards work everywhere upscale.
🌡️
Weather
Light, breathable clothes. Bring a layer for aggressive restaurant AC.
📱
Apps
Download Citymapper HK for transit. Google Translate camera works great here.

Local Tips & Spots

💰
Currency & Money
Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). $1 USD ≈ 16,000 IDR. Carry cash for local warungs, temples, and markets. ATMs widely available. Notify your bank before travel. Avoid exchange booths offering "too good" rates.
🛺
Getting Around
Use Grab or Gojek apps for reliable, metered rides. Avoid unmetered taxis. Rent a scooter only if you're experienced. Traffic in Kuta/Seminyak can be chaotic — budget extra time.
🍜
Food & Water
Stick to bottled water — don't drink tap. Try nasi goreng, mie goreng, satay, and babi guling. Warung local spots are cheaper and often delicious. Avoid salads at local spots if worried about stomach.
🛕
Temple Etiquette
Wear a sarong & sash to enter temples (usually rented at entrance). Cover shoulders and knees. Don't enter if menstruating. Remove shoes when entering. Respect ceremonies and don't interrupt.
🌺
Must-See Near Nusa Dua
Uluwatu Temple at sunset, Tanah Lot, Garuda Wisnu Kencana (GWK) Cultural Park, Jimbaran Bay seafood at sunset, Seminyak beach clubs (Ku De Ta, Potato Head), Tegallalang rice terraces.
🩺
Health & Safety
Bali is generally safe. Watch your belongings in busy markets. Drink only bottled water. Bring travel insurance. BIMC Hospital Nusa Dua is the closest quality medical facility to Grand Hyatt. Emergency: 112.
📡
Connectivity
Buy a local SIM at the airport (Telkomsel or XL Axiata) for ~$5-10 for data. Grand Hyatt has excellent WiFi. Download Google Maps offline before you go. WhatsApp works great for communication.
🕐
Time Zone
Bali is WITA — UTC+8. Denver (MDT) is UTC-6, so Bali is 14 hours ahead. When it's 8 AM in Bali, it's 6 PM the previous day in Denver. Plan calls home accordingly!
